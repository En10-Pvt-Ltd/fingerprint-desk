Act 1 · The problem, and the answer

the leak plays out

Seals record that a packet was opened. They cannot say who pressed the shutter.

In 2024, question papers for India's NEET medical entrance exam circulated before exam day. Investigators reported that an accomplice was let into a control room, opened the trunk holding the papers, photographed a paper, resealed the trunk and left. The pattern in paper leaks is consistent: someone with legitimate custody opens the packet, photographs a page with a phone, and messaging apps carry it from one handset to thousands in hours.

  1. Custody. A sealed packet reaches a fictional exam centre, Centre 0417, the night before the exam.
  2. The photograph. A custodian photographs one page, hand-held, under office light.
  3. The spread. WhatsApp recompresses and resends the photo. By morning the original phone no longer matters.

the page fights back

If every copy is invisibly different, the photograph itself names its source.

Once a paper is out, it cannot be un-leaked. So the goal here is different: make photographing a paper stop being anonymous. Each printed copy carries its own secret pattern of hair-width geometry shifts; the leaked photo, even after WhatsApp mangles it, still reads back the pattern of the one copy it came from.

It helps investigators, and, more importantly, it deters. And the accusation it produces is not a hunch: it arrives with a provable cap on the chance of ever blaming the wrong copy. That is what this page demonstrates, end to end, using the real pipeline outputs regenerated at build time.

Act 2 · The invisible fingerprint

Below are two full pages rendered by the encoder: one carries a fingerprint, one is an unmarked control. The fingerprint lives in geometry, not pixels or watermark patterns: selected word gaps are widened or narrowed by pixels (a hundredth of an inch at dpi), and the middle line of each three-line group is raised or lowered by pixels. Each copy gets its own pattern of shifts: its codeword. Color printers already hide invisible tracking dots on every page; this is the same instinct, but built to survive a bad phone photo and a trip through WhatsApp, and designed so a group of leakers cannot rub it out by comparing copies.

Act 4 · The guarantee

Attribution is only useful if it cannot smear the innocent. The system is engineered backwards from that requirement.

A one-in-a-million budget

The total probability of ever accusing an uninvolved copy is capped at per exam, split evenly between the two accusation paths ( each), across all copies and every fragment examined. False accusation is a budget, not a hope: no control page may ever cross the courtroom threshold, and the benchmark build fails if one does.

What a fragment can prove

Evidence scales with how much marked text the leak contains, and it comes graded, never as one headline number: a full paper supports a courtroom-strength identification even against a coalition, a page supports a strong claim against a small one, a single question points reliably at one source but cannot untangle a gang. The deliberate asymmetry runs through everything: better to miss a guilty party than to brand an innocent one. The table gives the largest symbol-error rate the channel may inflict while a fragment of each size still clears the courtroom budget, under the corrected hard-decision (flip) semantics.

The c column is the coalition size. A gang holding several copies cannot average the marks out of sheets of paper; without scanning, registering and reprinting, it can only splice: this page from one copy, that question from another. Every spliced piece still names the single copy it came from, and the coalition bound remains underneath as the safety net for anything genuinely mixed.

        of the accusation statistic

      

        Leaks rarely arrive as one clean page; they drip: a question today,
        a page next week. There is a trap here. If you ask "is this copy
        guilty yet?" after every new piece and keep asking, you will
        eventually accuse someone by chance, the way flipping enough coins
        eventually shows ten heads in a row. The fix is an anytime-valid
        e-process: a running evidence score built so you may check it after
        every fragment, forever, without ever inflating the false-accusation
        rate. A guilty copy's score climbs as its fragments arrive and it is
        accused the moment the score crosses the bar; an innocent copy's
        score essentially never gets near it, no matter how long you watch.

The sealed codebook

      

        There is an obvious objection to any secret code: how does a court
        know the authority did not invent it after seeing the leak, to frame
        a particular centre? The answer: before a single page is printed,
        the mapping from copies to codewords is sealed under a timestamped
        cryptographic commitment, an envelope that reveals nothing but
        cannot be swapped later. When a leak surfaces, the envelope is
        opened in front of the parties, and anyone can check that what was
        revealed matches what was sealed before the exam, then redo the
        accusation arithmetic themselves. The accusation is not "trust us";
        it is evidence a third party can verify.

Act 5 · Beyond exams

  

    The primitive is not exam-specific: any printed or per-recipient
    rendered page whose copies can differ can testify about its own leak,
    from the point in the custody chain where copies become distinct
    onward. These settings are gentler than the exam case in exactly the
    two ways that matter to the math: captures are cleaner (office scans
    and flatbeds instead of a rushed phone photo, so the symbol-error rate
    sits far below the table's tolerances), and each copy goes to a named
    recipient, so the plausible coalition is small and captures are
    typically at the c = 1 or c = 2 rows. No new machinery is claimed on
    any card; every figure is a row of the same corrected table.

What this cannot do

      
    
        
  • Retyping defeats any watermark. If the leaker
          transcribes the questions instead of photographing the page, the
          geometry is gone. The defense is speed and friction, not magic.
    • 
        
  • Pre-differentiation leaks are invisible. A leak
          from the press, or from any stage before copies are
          differentiated, carries no copy-specific fingerprint at all.
    • 
        
  • Single small fragments are investigative leads, not
          courtroom proof. A lone question's worth of text supports
          a lead; courtroom-tier accusations need the larger fragment scales
          in the Act 4 table.
    • 
        
  • Very small, tightly spaced text carries the mark
          poorly. The line carrier needs regular body text; in
          later bench tests, documents with line spacing under roughly
          12 points lost segmentation through the messaging channel, so
          such regions are excluded from marking rather than marked
          unreliably.
